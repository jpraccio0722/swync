params: [device, number, channel, lo, hi]
arities: [2, 3, 5]

A knob, a wheel or a pedal, as a signal you can use anywhere:

```swync
fn lead(n) = lowpass(saw(n.m2h), cc("push", 74), 1)
```

`cc("push", 74)` is controller 74 on channel 1, reading 0 to 1. The device is
a port's name — matched on any part of it, ignoring case — or its number. The
editor offers the ports this machine has as you type inside the quotes.

## A range at the call

A cutoff wants hertz, not a fraction, so say so once where the knob is named
rather than doing the arithmetic at every use:

```swync
cc("push", 74, 1, 200, 5000)
```

That is `device, number, channel, lo, hi`. The channel comes after the
controller number because which knob is the question you are answering, and
which channel is a detail most rigs never have to think about.

The range runs the other way round quite happily — `cc("push", 74, 1, 1, 0)`
gives you a knob that turns down.

## Why it is smoothed

A controller is seven bits arriving a few hundred times a second: a staircase.
Wired straight to a filter cutoff it zippers, and the zipper is the *steps*
rather than the speed. So the value slews to each new one over about ten
milliseconds — under the point where a knob stops feeling connected to the
hand turning it, and well over the point where the steps stop being audible.

A knob nobody has touched yet reads zero, which is the bottom of its range. A
port that is not connected reads zero too, and the program still runs.

## Related

`bend` is the pitch wheel, which rests in the middle rather than at the
bottom. `aftertouch` is how hard the keys are being leant on. `midiin` plays
the notes rather than reading the knobs.
