name: midiclock
params: [device]
arities: [1]
variadic: false
receives: number
returns: number
---

Send the transport's clock to a port, so a synth's own arpeggiator, delays and
sequencer line up with the part you wrote for it:

```swync
midiclock("deluge")
play(bass, midiout("deluge"), vel: [1, .6, .8])
```

Twenty-four ticks to the quarter note, plus **start** when the transport
starts and **stop** when it stops. There is no channel and no tempo to give
it: clock belongs to the transport, not to a part.

The device is a port's name — matched on any part of it, ignoring case — or
its number. Write it once per port; naming the same port twice would send it
every tick twice, which reads as double tempo at the other end.

Ticks are counted against **bar time** rather than against a stopwatch, so a
tempo change — including one under an `accel` — is followed without anything
here being told about it.

## Following, rather than sending

This is the sending half only. Following somebody *else's* clock is not in the
language, and that is deliberate: which synth a part is written for is a fact
about the piece and travels with it, but whether you are the slave tonight is
a fact about the rig. The same piece is the master in the studio and the slave
when the drummer's box is running the room.

So it is in the settings panel, under MIDI — **Follow clock**, beside the
ports it names. While one is being followed the tempo is theirs, and the panel
shows what is arriving.

If that clock stops — a cable out, or the other box stopped without saying so
— the transport **keeps playing** at the tempo it last saw, and the panel says
it has lost it. Silence in a room is worse than being out of sync, and the
last tempo is the best guess anybody has.
